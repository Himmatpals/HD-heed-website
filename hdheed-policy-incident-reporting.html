Incident Reporting Process

What HD HeeD workers must report immediately and who to contact.

Version 1.0Effective: 26 May 2026Review: 26 May 2027

Purpose

This policy makes sure staff report incidents, hazards, concerns and near misses quickly so HD HeeD can protect participants, support workers and meet NDIS obligations.

Workers must report immediately. HD HeeD will decide what records, follow-up, family communication and external reporting are required.

Report immediately

Contact HD HeeD immediately if any of the following occur or are suspected:

  • death, serious injury, injury, fall, head knock, burn, wound, choking, seizure or medical deterioration;
  • abuse, neglect, exploitation, rough handling, intimidation, threats or discrimination;
  • unlawful physical contact, sexual misconduct or any safeguarding concern;
  • medication error, missed medication, wrong dose, refusal or medication safety concern;
  • unauthorised restrictive practice or use of restraint without authorisation;
  • participant missing, absent unexpectedly or leaving in an unsafe way;
  • complaint from participant, family, representative, neighbour or other provider;
  • property damage, theft, money concern, privacy breach or conflict of interest;
  • unsafe home environment, aggressive animal, infection risk, fire/smoke hazard or manual handling risk;
  • near miss where harm could have occurred.
Emergency first: call 000 immediately if there is immediate danger, serious injury, urgent medical concern, fire, violence or police/ambulance need. Then notify HD HeeD as soon as safe.

Who to contact

  1. Emergency services: 000 for immediate danger or urgent medical/police/fire response.
  2. HD HeeD office: 02 8630 5500.
  3. HD HeeD email: info@hdheed.com.au for non-urgent follow-up or documents.
  4. Manager/RN on-call: add current on-call number before issuing to staff.
Do not wait until the end of the shift for serious incidents, injuries, abuse/neglect concerns, medication errors or unsafe situations.

What to record

As soon as safe, record clear facts only:

  • date, time and location;
  • who was involved and who witnessed it;
  • what happened, using factual language;
  • injury, symptoms, behaviour or risks observed;
  • action taken, who was contacted and when;
  • participant response and current safety status.

Do not blame, guess, diagnose or make unsupported comments.
