Module 1

Why infection control matters

Infection control protects participants, workers, families and the wider community. Many participants may be more vulnerable to infection because of disability, age, health conditions, wounds, reduced immunity, breathing problems or personal care needs.

  • Use standard precautions with every participant.
  • Assume blood and body fluids can carry infection.
  • Stop and ask for guidance if a task feels unsafe.
  • Report concerns early instead of waiting until the end of the shift.
Module 2

Hand hygiene

Hand hygiene is the most important infection control step. Use soap and water when hands are visibly dirty. Use alcohol-based hand rub when hands are not visibly dirty and it is available.

  • Clean hands before and after participant contact.
  • Clean hands before preparing food, assisting meals or medication prompting.
  • Clean hands after toileting support, personal care, cleaning, waste handling or touching body fluids.
  • Clean hands before putting on gloves and after removing gloves.
  • Gloves do not replace hand hygiene.
Module 3

PPE basics

PPE means personal protective equipment. It may include gloves, mask, apron/gown and eye protection depending on the task and risk.

  • Wear gloves for contact with blood, body fluids, wounds, continence care or contaminated items.
  • Change gloves between different tasks and between participants.
  • Wear a mask if directed, if there are respiratory symptoms, or if HD HeeD or public health advice requires it.
  • Wear an apron/gown if clothing may be contaminated.
  • Remove PPE safely and perform hand hygiene straight after removal.
Module 4

Respiratory hygiene and staying home when unwell

Workers must not attend shifts when they may spread infection. This protects participants and other staff.

  • Cover coughs and sneezes with a tissue or elbow.
  • Dispose of tissues promptly and clean hands afterwards.
  • Do not attend work if you have fever, vomiting, diarrhoea, flu-like symptoms, new cough, infectious rash, or have been told to isolate.
  • Contact HD HeeD as early as possible if you are unwell before a shift.
  • Tell HD HeeD if a participant appears unwell or reports infectious symptoms.
Module 5

Cleaning, waste and equipment

Keep the participant environment safe and clean within the duties of the shift. Follow the participant care plan and HD HeeD instructions.

  • Clean shared equipment before and after use if directed.
  • Do not use visibly dirty or damaged equipment; report it.
  • Dispose of gloves, masks and contaminated waste safely.
  • Handle soiled linen/clothing carefully and avoid shaking it.
  • Do not bring unnecessary personal items into personal care areas.
Module 6

What to report immediately

Report infection risks as soon as possible. Do not wait until the next day if there is a risk to the participant or others.

  • Participant has fever, flu-like symptoms, vomiting, diarrhoea or new respiratory symptoms.
  • You are unwell before or during a shift.
  • There is exposure to blood or body fluids.
  • PPE is unavailable when required.
  • There is a wound, infection concern, unsafe equipment or environmental risk.
  • Call emergency services for urgent or life-threatening concerns.
