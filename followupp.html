7-touch follow-up sequence

This is the core sequence. Keep the tone respectful, local, and helpful. The aim is to build referral trust, not pressure anyone.

Touch Timing Channel Purpose What to log
1 Day 0 Email Introduce HD HeeD as a local NDIS support option and ask permission to send a referral guide. Email sent, segment, suburb, service focus.
2 Day 2 Phone task or short email Confirm the right contact and ask what service gaps they see locally. Right person confirmed, wrong person, voicemail, no answer, call-back date.
3 Day 5 Email Send value: one-page referral guide, catchment, response time, intake criteria. Guide sent, attachment/link sent, any click/reply.
4 Day 7 Phone Main conversation day. Ask about referral gaps, waitlists, difficult-to-place supports, and what would make referral easier. Need identified, objections, meeting requested, follow-up promised.
5 Day 10 Email Reinforce local fit, responsiveness, clinical background, participant-first approach. Second follow-up sent, updated status.
6 Day 14 Short email or SMS only if appropriate Permission-based final nudge. Ask whether to keep them on local referral updates. Nurture yes/no, unsubscribe/no contact, next review date.
7 Monthly Email / relationship call Warm nurture: capacity update, local availability, helpful NDIS support tip, invitation to connect. Monthly touch sent, referrals received, relationship score.

Detailed call day conversation guide

This section gives deeper scripts for Day 2 and Day 7, including branching responses.

Universal call structure

1. Open respectfully: “Hi, this is [Your name] from HD HeeD. I know you may be busy, so I’ll be brief.”

2. Explain relevance: “We support NDIS participants around Richmond/Hawkesbury and Penrith, and I’m introducing ourselves to local referral partners.”

3. Ask permission: “Is now a suitable time for a two-minute introduction, or should I call another time?”

4. Find the right person: “Who is the best person to receive local NDIS provider information or referral pathway details?”

5. Ask one useful question: “What type of local support is hardest to find at the moment?”

6. Offer value: “I can send a one-page referral guide with our service area, intake process and contact details.”

7. Close clearly: “I’ll send that today and check back next week unless you prefer no follow-up.”

If they are busy

“No problem at all. I do not want to interrupt your day. Would it be better if I send a short email with our local referral details, and you can forward it to the right person if relevant?”

If yes: “Thank you. I’ll keep it brief. Is this the best email address?”

If no: “No worries. I’ll leave it there. Thank you for your time.”

If they ask what HD HeeD does

“HD HeeD is a local NDIS provider supporting participants across Richmond, Hawkesbury, Penrith and surrounding Western Sydney areas. We focus on practical, person-centred supports such as daily living support, personal care, community access, carer relief/respite and clinically informed coordination where appropriate. Our aim is to be responsive, easy to communicate with, and respectful of participant choice and control.”

If they say they already have providers

“That’s completely understandable. I’m not asking to replace any provider or create an exclusive arrangement. I’m simply introducing HD HeeD as another local option for times when your usual providers are full, not the right fit, or when a participant specifically wants a provider in the Richmond/Hawkesbury or Penrith area.”

If they ask for proof or experience

“The main value we bring is that we are locally focused and clinically led. We understand documentation, risk, communication with families, and the importance of reliable follow-up. We also understand that NDIS supports must respect participant choice and control. We are happy to start with a small, appropriate referral and demonstrate our responsiveness.”

If they may refer someone

“Thank you. To keep it safe and appropriate, please only send participant details through the correct referral pathway and with consent. For the first step, you can send general details about the support need, suburb, funding type and urgency, and we can confirm whether we are suitable before receiving detailed health information.”

Priority target groups from the research

Start with A1 and A2 contacts in Richmond, Windsor, Hawkesbury and Penrith. These are the strongest referral relationship categories.

A1 — Highest priority

  • NDIS Local Area Coordinator offices in Penrith and Windsor
  • Support coordinators and specialist support coordinators
  • Hospitals, discharge teams and community health intake
  • High-footfall GP clinics in Richmond, Windsor, Penrith and Kingswood
  • Disability providers that may have overflow, waitlist or reciprocal referral opportunities

A2 — Strong local partners

  • Allied health clinics: OT, physio, speech, exercise physiology
  • Plan managers with local client relationships
  • Community hubs and multicultural services
  • Employment services and school transition networks
  • Aged care and carer support organisations

Monthly nurture system

The goal is to stay useful without becoming annoying. Send only to warm contacts or contacts who have not opted out.

Monthly email structure

This month, we can assist with: - [Service/capacity 1] - [Service/capacity 2] - [Service/capacity 3] A quick reminder: we aim to respond quickly, communicate clearly, and support participant choice and control. If you have a participant or family looking for a local option, you are welcome to send a general enquiry first so we can confirm whether we are suitable. Kind regards, [Your name] HD HeeD [Phone] [Email] If you prefer not to receive these local referral updates, please reply “unsubscribe”.

Quarterly relationship call

“Hi [Name], this is [Your name] from HD HeeD. I’m just checking in because we connected earlier about local NDIS supports. I wanted to ask whether there are any current service gaps in Richmond/Hawkesbury or Penrith that your team is finding difficult to place. I’m not asking for anything urgent — just trying to keep our local support availability useful and realistic.”

Relationship-building ideas

  • Offer a simple referral pathway sheet, not a long brochure.
  • Invite coordinators to send a general suitability enquiry before full referral.
  • Give fast “yes/no/not suitable” responses so coordinators trust your communication.
  • Send thank-you updates after referrals, without breaching participant privacy.
  • Ask what support gaps exist instead of only promoting services.
  • Attend local disability networks, council inclusion events, carer groups and provider expos.

Compliance and safe outreach rules

Do not do this

  • Do not pressure support coordinators, clinicians or families.
  • Do not offer commissions or exclusive referral deals.
  • Do not add people to bulk SMS without permission.
  • Do not ignore unsubscribe or do-not-contact requests.
  • Do not store participant health details in a marketing spreadsheet.
  • Do not claim capacity, registration, services or response times that are not accurate.

Do this instead

  • Keep outreach professional, relevant and local.
  • Provide information that supports participant choice and control.
  • Use clear unsubscribe wording in marketing-style emails.
  • Record consent status and do-not-contact requests.
  • Separate referral-partner CRM from participant/client records.
  • Respond quickly and honestly when a referral is not suitable.

Recommended unsubscribe line

If you prefer not to receive follow-up emails from me about local referral pathways, please reply “unsubscribe” and I will update my records.

Participant information rule

Please only send participant details through the correct referral pathway and with consent. For the first suitability check, send general information only: suburb, support need, urgency, funding type and preferred contact process. Avoid sending detailed health information until consent and intake pathway are confirmed.
